    Table Strategies

    • Flat staking: keep each wager to the same small share of your balance to soften losing stretches.
    • Fixed-target discipline: pick a target that suits your style and avoid changing it after a loss.
    • Stop rules: set a win goal and a loss limit in advance, then step away once either is hit.
    • Never chase: increasing stakes to recover losses only widens the swing; variance does not respond to pressure.
    • Practice first: test any staking plan on the free table before using real funds.

    Guest Questions

    1. What exactly counts as a win?

    The roll must land strictly below your target. If you choose 50, then 1 to 49 wins and 50 or above loses the stake.

    2. How is the payout multiplier set?

    It reflects the real odds of your target with the house margin removed. Lower targets mean fewer winning numbers and therefore a larger multiplier.

    3. Is there a best target to choose?

    No target changes the expected return. High targets smooth the ride with frequent small wins; lower targets trade frequency for size. Choose by temperament, not guesswork.

    4. Do I need to play with real money?

    No. The practice table uses complimentary chips, mirrors the paid version closely and is the better place to learn before any deposit.

    5. Can a staking system guarantee profit?

    No staking plan can beat a negative-edge random game. Systems only shape variance; their value lies in helping you manage your bankroll.
